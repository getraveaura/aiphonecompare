**The short answer:** A live virtual receptionist costs far more than an AI receptionist once you price your real call volume. At 150 three-minute calls a month, the cheapest complete published plan from Dialzara, Smith.ai's AI receptionist and AgentZap runs **$199 to $295**. The same 150 calls on Abby Connect, Smith.ai's live agents and Ruby runs **$1,347.75 to $1,635**. A person still earns the extra money on some calls, and the stories below show which ones.

Want your own number? Put your calls and call length into the [cost calculator](/cost/calculator/).

## What "virtual receptionist" means here

A **live virtual receptionist** is a real person who answers your calls from somewhere else: Ruby, Smith.ai's live agents, or Abby Connect's human answering. An **AI receptionist** is software that answers, talks to the caller, takes a message or books a time. Some companies sell both. Smith.ai has a separate AI plan and a live-agent plan. Abby Connect sells human, AI or a mix on the same plans.

## How each one bills (and why that changes your bill)

### Live services bill a person's minutes (or calls)

Ruby and Abby Connect sell bundles of minutes, so a 3-minute call uses 3 of them. Smith.ai's live agents are the exception: they bill by the call, so a 3-minute call and a 6-minute call cost the same on the plan price.

### AI services bill minutes, calls or callers

Dialzara and AgentZap bill by the minute. Smith.ai's AI receptionist bills by the call. Goodcall bills by unique caller, so the same person calling twice counts once. We don't turn that into a per-call price, so Goodcall is not in the tables.

### Calls vs minutes, worked once

50 calls × 3 minutes = 150 minutes. That is three times Ruby's 50-minute starting plan. Every live starting plan on this page is outgrown by 50 calls a month.

## Why live gets expensive faster as calls grow

**A live service charges for a person's time, so every extra call adds paid minutes (or a paid call).** AI plans charge too, but at a much lower rate per minute or call. You can see the gap in the tables. Going from 50 to 300 calls, Ruby's cost rises from $620 to $3,127.50 and Abby Connect's from $492 to $2,484. Over the same jump, Dialzara goes from $72.20 to $349 and AgentZap from $109 to $632.50. At 300 calls, the cheapest live figure in Table B ($2,100, Smith.ai live agents) is still more than three times the highest AI figure in Table A ($632.50). If you're weighing Ruby on its own, see [Ruby alternatives with published prices](/alternatives/ruby/).

## When a live person is worth the extra

**A person is worth paying for when a wrong answer costs more than the monthly gap, as with emergencies, upset callers, and callers who won't talk to a machine.** Here is what the owners and sources in our stories actually said. (Each story links to its original source.)

**Businesses that turned AI off:**

- **[The Blissful Clean](/stories/blissful-clean-removed-ai/)** (cleaning, Grand Rapids). Kyleigh Lipsitz put an AI assistant on after-hours calls so evening and weekend quote requests wouldn't go to voicemail. She said a few customers told her they would cancel because they hate talking to the robot, and she described a pause while the AI was still thinking. She removed it and went back to voicemail, with a person calling back the next day.
- **[Red Dog's Roofing](/stories/red-dogs-roofing-stopped-ai/)**. CEO Patrick Cochran said customers did not give good feedback when their after-hours answering service started using AI, so the company asked the service to stop. He said he still prefers a person.
- **[The Thorpe Practice](/stories/thorpe-practice-sheffield-emma-switched-off/)** (an NHS GP practice in Sheffield, England). Residents told a local paper that its AI receptionist, Emma, made the practice harder to reach, especially for elderly and less confident callers. Some patients said online it booked appointments that didn't exist. The practice said many patients found Emma helpful, but it switched Emma off after significant feedback. Calls have gone to the reception team since 5 November 2026, and the practice warned of longer waits at busy times.

**What 125 reviews said:** Hire Bloom read 10,938 Google reviews of HVAC, pest control, plumbing, electrical and roofing companies and found 125 that mentioned AI answering ([our summary](/stories/home-services-review-roundup/)). It says 78% were negative, 16% neutral and 6% positive. The reviews that ended in a cancelled job or a warning to others were mostly emergencies where the AI stood between the caller and a person. The worst pattern was AI confirming a slot the company couldn't keep. Every positive review described an after-hours booking that a person honored. Hire Bloom sells human agents, so it isn't neutral on this.

**Where AI worked:**

- **[A Kent heating firm's 90-day test](/stories/kent-heating-firm-90-day-test/)**. A four-engineer boiler repair firm with no office staff picked up 61 calls that would have gone unanswered: 43 booked on the call, 12 became callbacks closed within 24 hours, and 6 callers hung up once they realized it wasn't a person. The author says complaints and refund requests went badly, emergencies needed a rule sending "leak", "no heat" or "emergency" calls straight to the on-call engineer, and saying plainly in the greeting that it was an AI assistant cut hang-ups.
- **[Ghaffari Law Firm](/stories/ghaffari-eve-intake/)**. In a case study from the vendor, Eve, the personal-injury firm's AI intake agent answers every call and can warm-transfer a qualified caller to staff. The study says missed calls went from about 20% to zero.

**The pattern:** people did better with emergencies, complaints, older or hesitant callers, and any booking the business has to honor. AI did well on routine questions and bookings, especially when it hands the hard calls to a person. For more, see [is an AI receptionist worth it?](/worth-it/)

## Mixing both: AI first, a person for some calls

**Yes, you can mix them, and that often costs less than all-live.** Let AI answer every call and pass some to a person. A rough way to price it:

> **Monthly cost ≈ AI plan for all your calls + live minutes (or live calls) for the share handed to a person**

Two things to check before you rely on that. At Abby Connect, AI and human answering share one plan, and an AI-answered minute counts as half an Abby Minute. Smith.ai's AI page says the AI can transfer a call to a live receptionist, but that page doesn't print a price for it, so we don't add one. Ask for that price in writing before you sign.

## Common questions

**How much does a live virtual receptionist cost per month?**
Live virtual receptionists bill for a person's time, by the minute or by the call. As checked on 20 November 2026, Ruby starts at $250 a month for 50 receptionist minutes, Abby Connect's Starter plan is $164 a month for 50 Abby Minutes, and Smith.ai's live agents start at $300 a month for 30 calls. At 3 minutes a call, Ruby's starting plan covers 16 calls.

**Is an AI receptionist cheaper than a live virtual receptionist?**
On published prices, yes, by a wide margin. At 150 three-minute calls a month, the cheapest complete plan from Dialzara, Smith.ai's AI receptionist and AgentZap runs $199 to $295 (checked 20 November 2026). The same 150 calls on Abby Connect, Smith.ai's live agents and Ruby run $1,347.75 to $1,635, and Ruby can be higher because its minutes include hold time and after-call work.

**What does an AI receptionist cost at 50, 150 and 300 calls a month?**
At 3-minute calls, using each company's published plans (checked 20 November 2026): at 50 calls, $72.20 (Dialzara), $75 (Smith.ai AI) or $109 (AgentZap, plus a $499 setup fee). At 150 calls, $199 (Dialzara), $270 (Smith.ai AI) or $295 (AgentZap). At 300 calls, $349 (Dialzara), $500 (Smith.ai AI) or $632.50 (AgentZap). These are worked from list prices, not quotes. Taxes and unpublished fees are not included.

**What does a live virtual receptionist cost at 50, 150 and 300 calls?**
At 3-minute calls (checked 20 November 2026): at 50 calls, $492 (Abby Connect), $530 (Smith.ai live) or $620 (Ruby). At 150 calls, $1,347.75 (Abby Connect), $1,440 (Smith.ai live) or $1,635 (Ruby). At 300 calls, $2,100 (Smith.ai live), $2,484 (Abby Connect) or $3,127.50 (Ruby).

**Are there setup fees on either side?**
Of the six services here, only AgentZap prints one: $499 one-time. Dialzara, Smith.ai's live agents, Ruby and Abby Connect say on their pricing pages that they charge none. Smith.ai's AI pricing page doesn't say, so we list it as not published.
